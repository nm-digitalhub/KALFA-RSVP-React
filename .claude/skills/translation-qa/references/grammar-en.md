# English grammar checks

Use when English is the target. Most of these are places where Hebrew leaves out something English must state.

## Articles

Hebrew marks only "the", so a translator has to supply `a` and `an`, and decide when no article is right. Ask two questions: does this mean this exact one, one of many, or all of them everywhere? Is the noun countable?

- First mention takes `a` or `an`. Later mentions of the same thing take `the`.
- Non-count nouns take no article for a general statement, and `the` for a specific one.
- A plural noun used for a whole group takes no article.
- `a` and `an` follow the sound, not the letter: `an honest error`, `a union`, `a U.S. ship`.

## Words Hebrew leaves out

- Add the verb "to be" where Hebrew has none: `הדלת סגורה` becomes `The door is closed`.
- Use "have" for possession: `יש לי` becomes `I have`.
- English needs a stated subject, including where Hebrew drops it.

## Word order and tense

- Modern Hebrew often places an indefinite subject after the verb. Move it before the verb in English.
- Hebrew has one past, one present and one future form, so English tense and aspect must come from context. Read how the string is used before choosing, for example, between `was saved` and `has been saved`.

## Addressing people

- Use singular `they` for one person whose gender is unknown. It takes a plural verb: `they are`.
- Hebrew gender often disappears in English. Check that a sentence still shows who is meant.

## Plurals

- Plural keys need `_one` and `_other`. In English, 0 uses `_other`.
